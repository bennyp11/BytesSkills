# Your next favorite thing might not look like you

Status: candidate development route, not a finished script.

**Seed:** We are slowly removing coincidences from our lives.

**Topic:** Tech

**Format:** reductions v0.1.0

**Audience:** General audience (carried forward and reconfirmed in intake).

**Creative direction:** Accumulate recognizable examples into a realization, wonder and “wow, that is weird”; not an alarmist warning or ragebait.

**Angle:** Explore the odd circularity of selecting future experiences using an established picture of who you are.

**Example progression:**

1. Choose music because it resembles what you already play.
2. Choose a holiday around interests you already list.
3. Choose an activity because it matches your stated preferences.
4. Recall a hobby or fascination that began when someone persuaded you to try something you thought was not your thing.

**Realization:** Convenient matches help the current version of you, while a mismatch can sometimes create a new version.

**Possible landing before the format's follow CTA:** What if the thing you would love most is something you would never think to ask for?

**Qualification:** This is a question about discovery, not proof that personalization prevents growth. People and recommendation systems can deliberately explore.

**Claim handling:** Treat the seed as an evocative premise about opportunities for serendipity, not a literal established trend that technology eliminates coincidences. Examples are illustrative possibilities, not measured prevalence claims. Verify any empirical claims or product-specific behavior added during scripting.
