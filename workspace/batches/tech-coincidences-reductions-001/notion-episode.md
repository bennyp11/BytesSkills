## Classification
Standalone episode. Not part of the 100K in 90 Days challenge.
Format: Reductions. Audience: general audience. Tone: wonder and recognition, not ragebait or an alarmist warning.
## Opening seed
> We’re slowly removing coincidences from our lives.
## Selected concept
A day without a wrong turn, with a personal reflection on how important experiences can begin with an accident.
## Development beats
1. Walk through an ordinary day: check the weather, follow the fastest route, choose a cafe from reviews, and watch something recommended for you.
2. Each choice makes sense on its own. Together they can smooth away some of the detours where unexpected experiences happen.
3. Invite the viewer to think of a friendship, favorite place or interest that began with an unplanned encounter. Use hypothetical examples unless Ben supplies a true personal story.
4. Let the pattern land as a curious realization rather than a demand to abandon technology.
## Possible landing
> How much of your life began with something that wasn’t the best option?
Alternative: You can plan the trip. You can’t always plan which part you’ll remember.
## Qualification
Technology does not literally eliminate coincidence. It changes opportunities for chance encounters, and can create new ones too. Treat the opening as an evocative premise, not a measured universal trend.
## Production direction
Narrator on camera, hook text, manually timed speech captions, dead air removed, restrained sound effects. Light explanatory animation is optional; style and actual sound assets are not selected yet.
## Next step
Develop hook options and a script. End with the format’s helpfulness-based follow CTA. No final script, publication date or winning trial has been selected.
## Why this concept
Everyday examples make the pattern easy to see; the personal reflection gives the realization emotional weight.
