# The stranger who used to choose for you

Status: candidate development route, not a finished script.

**Seed:** We are slowly removing coincidences from our lives.

**Topic:** Tech

**Format:** reductions v0.1.0

**Audience:** General audience (carried forward and reconfirmed in intake).

**Creative direction:** Accumulate recognizable examples into a realization, wonder and “wow, that is weird”; not an alarmist warning or ragebait.

**Angle:** Notice that some apparently random discoveries came from being exposed to another person’s taste.

**Example progression:**

1. A song someone else played in the car.
2. A book left behind in a holiday rental.
3. A film a friend picked that you would never have chosen.
4. Compare those moments with asking a service for something specifically suited to you.

**Realization:** Knowing what you already like is different from encountering something that changes what you like.

**Possible landing before the format's follow CTA:** Some of your favorite things had to get past the person you used to be.

**Qualification:** Personalized systems can introduce unfamiliar things too. Contrast different kinds of discovery rather than claiming recommendations only repeat existing tastes.

**Claim handling:** Treat the seed as an evocative premise about opportunities for serendipity, not a literal established trend that technology eliminates coincidences. Examples are illustrative possibilities, not measured prevalence claims. Verify any empirical claims or product-specific behavior added during scripting.
