# Look backward from the thing that mattered

Status: candidate development route, not a finished script.

**Seed:** We are slowly removing coincidences from our lives.

**Topic:** Tech

**Format:** reductions v0.1.0

**Audience:** General audience (carried forward and reconfirmed in intake).

**Creative direction:** Accumulate recognizable examples into a realization, wonder and “wow, that is weird”; not an alarmist warning or ragebait.

**Angle:** Begin with meaningful parts of a life, then trace them back to ordinary accidents.

**Example progression:**

1. A friendship that started because two people sat next to each other.
2. A favorite place discovered after missing a turn.
3. An interest sparked by the wrong video or an unexpected conversation.
4. Then notice the little inconveniences we routinely use technology to avoid.

**Realization:** We can recognize the value of an accident after it happens, but not necessarily when deciding whether to prevent its setup.

**Possible landing before the format's follow CTA:** You can plan the trip. You cannot always plan which part you will remember.

**Qualification:** Use hypothetical examples unless personal stories are supplied. Avoid implying every inconvenience is valuable or that preventing one proves a specific future was lost.

**Claim handling:** Treat the seed as an evocative premise about opportunities for serendipity, not a literal established trend that technology eliminates coincidences. Examples are illustrative possibilities, not measured prevalence claims. Verify any empirical claims or product-specific behavior added during scripting.
